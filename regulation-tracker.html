    Payment institutions: licensing, governance, remote onboarding, cybersecurity and account caps

    Circular No. 2026-10

    Replaces Circular 2018-16 as the operating rulebook for payment institutions. Keeps the three account levels capped at TND 1,500, 5,000 and 20,000, dinar-only and domestic-only activity, and distribution (not issuance) of e-money. Adds a board audit and risk committee, independent control functions, client-fund segregation at a bank, prior BCT approval of partnerships, remote onboarding with liveness checks and two-factor authentication, annual ANCS-certified security audits and immediate incident reporting.

    Published 25 Sept 2026; corrected version posted 28 Sept. Enters into force three months after publication, i.e. late December 2026.

    In force from: 2026-12 (approx.)Replaces: Circular No. 2018-16 of 31 December 2018

    School registration fees for 2026-2027 payable only through digital channels

    Circular note No. 2026-202

    Note addressed to banks, the Office National des Postes and payment institutions stating that school registration fees for 2026-2027 are to be paid exclusively through digital channels: mobile wallets and bank or postal payment cards. It is a state nudge that pushes first-time users, including La Poste customers, towards wallets and cards, and gives payment institutions a mass-market use case.

    Applies to the 2026-2027 school-year registration, as reported by the press; full text not reviewed.

  5. May 5, 2026
    In forceBCT circular · BCT

    TUNPAY: single national label for mobile payment

    Note No. 2026-132

    Creates TUNPAY, a common label for mobile payment designed with the Société Monétique Tunisie, which runs the national mobile switch. Wallet issuers (banks, La Poste and payment institutions) are asked to display it in their apps, and merchants and agents at points of acceptance. It does not set fees. Its practical impact for fintechs depends on how access to the SMT switch is organised.

    Issued 5 May 2026. A branding and trust standard, not a technical interoperability rule; SMT access terms for independent fintechs remain to be clarified.

  8. December 12, 2025
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    2026 Finance Law: resident foreign-currency accounts and repeal of the TND 5,000 cash rule

    Law No. 2025-17 (Finance Law 2026)

    An amendment adopted on 2 December 2025 (69 votes to 17) lets residents open foreign-currency or convertible-dinar accounts at authorised banks without prior BCT approval; accounts cannot be overdrawn. Article 54 repeals, from 1 January 2026, the 2019 rule that penalised cash settlement above TND 5,000 for sales of property, businesses and vehicles. Practical use of the FX accounts depends on BCT implementing rules.

    In force since 1 January 2026. Mid-2026 reports said the BCT implementing circulars for resident FX accounts had not yet been issued; we could not confirm whether they have since.

    In parliamentBill · Parliament

    New Foreign Exchange Code bill (MPs' proposal)

    Proposed law on the Foreign Exchange Code (91 articles)

    Filed by MP Maher Ktari with about 45 co-sponsors to replace the 1976 FX law. It redefines residence, allows resident foreign-currency accounts and accounts for platform income aimed at freelancers, eases investment abroad, and covers digital assets and remote payments. The committee text adds an approved exchange operator status open to eligible startups, declaration and repatriation of foreign and crypto assets above a threshold, and prison terms of up to three years.

    In the Finance Committee. Vote postponed pending BCT input; in July 2026 the Finance Ministry proposed delaying to early 2027 and the committee planned to resume in October 2026. No committee or plenary vote found as of 10 Oct 2026.

  12. 2025-07
    In parliamentBill · Parliament

    Personal data protection bill to replace Organic Law 2004-63

    Draft organic law on personal data protection (132 articles)

    A 132-article text in six chapters that would repeal the 2004 law. It creates an independent authority with financial autonomy, raises fines for serious breaches to up to TND 200,000, introduces impact assessments for high-risk processing and a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions, adds articles on AI and rewrites rules on transfers abroad. Fintechs using biometrics, credit scoring or foreign cloud hosting would be directly affected.

    Filed by 17 MPs in July 2025. Rights and Freedoms Committee hearings in February 2026; on 20 May 2026 it discussed hearing more experts. No vote found as of October 2026.

  13. May 21, 2025
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Employment contracts: open-ended contract as default and ban on labour subcontracting

    Law No. 2025-9 of 21 May 2025

    Makes the open-ended contract (CDI) the default; fixed-term contracts are allowed only for temporary activity increases, replacements and seasonal work, otherwise they convert to CDIs. Probation is capped at six months, renewable once. Labour subcontracting is banned; service contracts are lawful only for specialised work outside the client's core activity and not under its control. Fines reach TND 10,000, doubled for companies. Startups using rolling CDDs or embedded outsourced developers are exposed.

    Published in JORT No. 61 on 23 May 2025. Employers had three months to regularise contracts.

  14. February 28, 2025
    In forceBCT circular · BCT

    Banks: minimum requirements for electronic customer onboarding (e-KYC)

    Circular No. 2025-06

    Six chapters and 18 articles allowing banks to onboard customers remotely through an automated or semi-automated process, provided identity assurance is at least equivalent to an in-person check under Circular 2017-08. Banks must apply risk-based due diligence, may use facial recognition and biometrics, must keep false-acceptance rates low, encrypt KYC files, test technology before deployment and keep a performance register. It underpins bank-fintech digital onboarding partnerships.

    Published 28 February 2025; applies to banks.

  17. November 11, 2024
    In forceCMF regulation · CMF

    Equity crowdfunding: platform approval and operating rules

    CMF regulation of 11 November 2024 on securities crowdfunding

    Completes Law 2020-37 and Decree 2022-765 for the securities track. Platforms need CMF approval, TND 100,000 paid-up capital, a business plan, AML/CFT controls, IT security, insurance and a data protection authorisation; the CEO must reside in Tunisia. Issuers publish a short information note on a CMF template instead of a prospectus, and canvassing outside the platform is banned. Stockbrokers and fund managers may operate platforms on prior declaration.

    Announced by the CMF as published in the Official Journal; completes the equity crowdfunding framework.

  18. August 2, 2024
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Cheque reform: real-time verification, chequebook limits and TuniChèque

    Law No. 2024-41 of 2 August 2024

    Reforms the cheque regime to restore the cheque as an immediate means of payment rather than informal credit. It introduces a new cheque format, limits on chequebooks and the TuniChèque platform, which lets a payee check fund availability in real time. In 2025 the number of cheques fell 67.5%, bills of exchange rose and part of the activity moved to transfers and cash, leaving a gap for digital instalment products.

    Main provisions in force since 2 February 2025.

  20. January 29, 2024
    Status unclearBCT circular · BCT

    Temporary reduction of bank fees for low-income customers

    Circular No. 2024-03

    Requires banks to lower six commissions, including account maintenance, card subscription, outgoing transfers and card-terminal payments, for customers with net monthly income of TND 1,500 or less, for one year. Press reports put the capped account fee at TND 36 a year, down from TND 68. It targets cost as a barrier to account ownership and benchmarks the pricing fintech wallets compete with.

    Exceptional one-year measure; banks could not raise tariffs until 31 December 2024 and had to adopt a compliant pricing policy. Whether any element was extended is not confirmed.

    Cybersecurity: creation and missions of the National Cybersecurity Agency (ANCS)

    Decree-law No. 2023-17 of 11 March 2023

    National cybersecurity framework. It creates the ANCS, replacing the former national computer security agency (ANSI), under the ministry for communication technologies, and gives it oversight of information system security in public and private bodies, standards, incident response and the accreditation of security auditors. BCT Circular 2026-10 relies on it: payment institutions need ANCS-certified audits and must report attacks to the ANCS.

    In forceDecree · Government

    Securities crowdfunding: limits for projects and contributors

    Decree No. 2022-765 of 19 October 2022

    Implements the securities track of Law 2020-37. A project may raise at most TND 1 million on a platform and each contributor may invest at most TND 10,000 per project. Companies listed on the stock exchange or that have made a CMF-approved public offer are excluded. Contributors can be residents or non-residents, including the diaspora, and funds sit in a ring-fenced account at a bank or La Poste.

    In force; completed by the CMF regulation of 11 November 2024.

  29. August 6, 2020
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Crowdfunding law: equity, loans and donations

    Law No. 2020-37 of 6 August 2020

    Defines crowdfunding as raising money from the public through an online platform for a project carried out in Tunisia, in three categories: securities (approved by the CMF), loans (approved by the BCT Governor) and donations (supervised by the ACM). Operators must be Tunisian public limited companies running only one category, and a project cannot be listed on two platforms of the same category at once.

    In force; implementing texts issued 2022-2024. First reported approval: donation platform CnBees, licensed by the ACM in December 2025.

  30. January 21, 2020
    In forceOther · BCT

    BCT regulatory sandbox for fintech

    BCT fintech regulatory sandbox

    Supervised test environment, launched with World Bank support, letting fintechs try innovative services with volunteer customers before holding a licence. The first cohort tested remote identification, cross-border clearing, crypto-assets and central bank digital currency over nine months. It came with a BCT-Fintech committee. It is not a licence: commercial activity still requires authorisation under Law 2016-48 and compliance with Circular 2026-10.

    Announced by communiqué on 11 Dec 2019, launched 21 Jan 2020; first cohort of four selected in late 2020/January 2021. No published outcomes or later cohort calls found.

    Circular No. 2019-02

    Payment institutions: operating rules and tiered payment accounts (2018 framework)

    Circular No. 2018-16 of 31 December 2018

    First operating rulebook for payment institutions created by Law 2016-48. It introduced tiered payment accounts with balance caps of TND 1,500, 5,000 and 20,000, and required an annual IT security audit sent to the BCT, a tested business continuity plan and management of operational and cyber risk.   36. April 17, 2018
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Startup Act: Startup Label and incentives for founders, startups and investors

    Law No. 2018-20 of 17 April 2018

    Creates the Startup Label for companies under eight years old, with fewer than 100 staff and turnover or balance sheet below TND 15 million. Benefits include founder leave and a one-year grant, corporate tax exemption and state-paid social contributions during the label, a foreign-currency account, patent support and investor tax incentives. The label is not a financial licence: payment or lending activity still needs BCT approval.

    In force, implemented by Decree 2018-840; 1,352 labelled startups by July 2026. A Startup Act 2.0 is in preparation.

    Circular No. 2017-08

    BCT circular setting customer identification and due diligence rules for supervised institutions under the AML/CFT law.   40. July 11, 2016
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Banks and financial institutions law, including payment institutions and Islamic banking

    Law No. 2016-48 of 11 July 2016

    Core banking law. It sets two-stage bank licensing by a committee chaired by the BCT governor, doubles minimum bank capital to TND 50 million, allows à la carte licences, creates a deposit guarantee fund, provides the first framework for Islamic banking and windows, and creates payment institutions (article 10 on services, article 20 on authorisation). There is no separate digital-bank licence, so neobanks need a full licence or the payment institution route.

    In force; legal basis for bank and payment institution licences.

  42. August 7, 2015
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing law

    Organic Law No. 2015-26 of 7 August 2015, amended by Organic Law No. 2019-9 of 23 January 2019

    Requires banks, payment institutions, brokers and other obliged entities to identify customers, monitor transactions and report suspicions to the CTAF, Tunisia's financial intelligence unit, via the goAML platform since 2021. Payment institutions' suspicious transaction reports rose from 4 in 2023 to 119 in 2024.     In forceDecree-law · Presidency

    Microfinance institutions and creation of the ACM supervisor

    Decree-Law No. 2011-117 of 5 November 2011

    Organises microfinance through two families of lenders: licensed microfinance companies set up as public limited companies, and microfinance associations. It creates the Autorité de Contrôle de la Microfinance (ACM), an independent supervisor that licenses institutions and publishes sector data. The ACM also supervises donation-based crowdfunding. Fintechs building lending or credit-scoring tools for microfinance work within this framework.

    In force; since late 2025 the ACM sets the reference rates used to judge excessive microcredit pricing.

  45. 2004
  46. July 27, 2004
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Personal data protection law

    Organic Law No. 2004-63 of 27 July 2004

    Governs personal data processing, supervised by the INPDP. Most processing must be declared in advance; some needs prior authorisation. Transfers abroad require INPDP authorisation and an adequate destination, and unauthorised transfers carry one year in prison and a TND 5,000 fine. Disclosure to third parties needs express written consent. Fintechs hosting data on foreign cloud services or using biometrics are directly concerned.

    In force, with Decrees 2007-3003 and 2007-3004. The INPDP has reportedly not operated since 2024; a replacement bill is in Parliament.

  47. 1976
  48. January 21, 1976
    In forceLaw · Parliament

    Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Code

    Law No. 76-18 of 21 January 1976

    Tunisia's foreign exchange regime, built on prior BCT authorisation for many operations involving foreign currency or assets held abroad. For startups it complicates paying for foreign SaaS and cloud, receiving foreign revenue and opening subsidiaries abroad. It is also the main legal constraint on crypto: buying or holding crypto abroad can amount to an FX offence, as there is no dedicated crypto law.

    In force, updated once in 1993 and supplemented by BCT circulars and the 2026 Finance Law. Replacement bill in committee.
