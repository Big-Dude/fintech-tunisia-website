International Technology Card in Tunisia: Limits, Uses, How to Get It

The carte technologique internationale is how most Tunisians pay for software, hosting, ads and online courses abroad. Who can get one, how much you can spend, what it can and can't be used for, and the alternatives.

Tunisia's dinar is not convertible, so a normal Tunisian bank card works only inside the country. To pay for a domain name, a cloud server, a software licence or an online course abroad, residents need a special card loaded with foreign currency: the international technology card (carte technologique internationale, or CTI). This guide explains the limits, who can get one, what you can buy with it, and the alternatives. It draws on the product pages of BIAT, ATB and Banque de Tunisie, and on two detailed 2026 guides by Wassim Gatri and NovaHoster.

What is the international technology card?

It is a payment card issued by a Tunisian bank or La Poste and funded in dinars, which the issuer converts to foreign currency when you pay a foreign website. The Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT) created the scheme in 2015 and set the current rules in Circular 2016-09, later extended to startups by Circular 2019-02. The yearly amount is a foreign-exchange allowance: once you have spent it, you can't top up until the next calendar year.

What is the limit of the carte technologique?

Holder Yearly limit Notes
Individual (resident Tunisian) TND 1,000 One allowance per person, across all banks
Company TND 10,000 Some banks restrict it to IT, telecoms, education, consulting or research firms
Company with the Startup label TND 100,000 Under the Startup Act

The allowance runs for the calendar year, according to BIAT, and unused amounts do not carry over. At about 3.36 dinars per euro (see the euro to dinar rate today), TND 1,000 is roughly €300 a year, or about €25 a month: enough for hosting and a few subscriptions, not for a full set of professional tools. Raising the individual limit has been announced several times since 2022, but no new circular has changed it so far.

Who can get one?

For individuals, banks require Tunisian nationality, residence in Tunisia and at least the baccalauréat or an equivalent diploma, according to ATB and the 2026 guides. In practice banks issue it to adults. Companies apply in their own name, and startups must show their label decision.

How do you apply?

  1. Choose an issuer. Most banks offer it (BIAT, ATB, BT, BTK, STB, Banque Zitouna, among others), as does La Poste, which requires an active postal current account (CCP).
  2. Bring the documents: your national ID card (CIN), a certified copy of your baccalauréat or higher diploma, and a sworn statement (déclaration sur l'honneur), usually signed at the branch. Companies add their trade register extract and statutes. ATB asks applicants to open a dedicated account first.
  3. Load the card in dinars at the branch or through online banking. The issuer converts at its own exchange rate when you pay.
  4. Keep your invoices. You may be asked to justify payments, and lending or selling the card breaks your sworn statement.

Fees vary by issuer and are often not published. The guides report a TND 20 annual fee at Banque Zitouna and a TND 3 renewal at La Poste; ask your bank about the issuance fee and the foreign-exchange commission before you choose.

What can you pay with it?

The BCT circular lists the services allowed. They include:

  • Website and app hosting, domain names and cloud services
  • Online advertising (search and social media ads)
  • Software licences and app development tools
  • Subscriptions to foreign websites, including freelance platforms, databases and educational sites
  • Online training and courses

Excluded: buying physical goods, living expenses, betting and prohibited sites. Some issuers filter by merchant category, so a payment can be declined even when it seems eligible. University tuition and visa fees are not explicitly listed; check with your bank.

Can you use PayPal in Tunisia?

Only in a very limited way. Foreign exchange rules stop residents from holding and withdrawing foreign-currency balances freely, so PayPal is not a practical way for Tunisians to get paid, and the technology card's allowance is too small for most freelancers' needs anyway. A bill tabled by eleven MPs, reported by Business News in August 2026, would recognise freelance work and open access to international payment platforms. But it also proposes a 15% levy on withdrawals and a USD 5,000 monthly cap. It has not been passed.

To get paid from abroad, see our guide to receiving money in Tunisia. The wider foreign exchange code reform could eventually loosen these rules, but it is still before Parliament.

Which option fits you?

  • Student or individual developer: the TND 1,000 card covers hosting, a domain and a few small subscriptions. La Poste is often the simplest option if you already have a CCP.
  • Freelancer or agency: apply as a company for the TND 10,000 allowance, and check your bank's sector list first.
  • Startup: get the Startup label to unlock TND 100,000 a year and a foreign-currency account.
  • Tourist travel: the separate tourist allowance (TND 6,000 a year) is for trips abroad, not online subscriptions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the limit of the carte technologique internationale?

TND 1,000 per calendar year for individuals, TND 10,000 for companies and TND 100,000 for companies with the Startup label. Unused amounts do not carry over to the next year.

Who can get an international technology card in Tunisia?

Tunisian residents with at least the baccalauréat or an equivalent diploma, and resident companies. You apply at a bank or La Poste with your ID card, a certified copy of your diploma and a signed sworn statement.

Can I buy products on foreign websites with the technology card?

No. The card is for services such as hosting, advertising, software, subscriptions and online training. Buying physical goods, living expenses and betting are excluded.

Can La Poste issue a technology card?

Yes. La Poste Tunisienne issues the international technology card to holders of an active postal current account (CCP), who can manage it through e-dinar.
