Euro to Tunisian Dinar Rate: How It Works and How to Get the Best

How many dinars you get for a euro depends less on luck than on where you change it. The euro to Tunisian dinar exchange rate is set on a managed interbank market, and every bank, exchange office and transfer app then adds its own margin. This guide explains how the EUR TND exchange rate works, what moved it in 2025 and 2026, the rules for travellers and Tunisians abroad, and how to get the best rate. How is the Tunisian dinar exchange rate set?

The dinar is neither pegged nor freely floating. Its rate is determined on the interbank foreign exchange market, created in March 1994, where Tunisian banks trade currencies with each other. The Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT) is the main market maker and, in its own words, "intervenes to regulate market liquidity", according to a 2023 paper by the Observatoire Tunisien de l'Économie.

The regime has loosened in stages. The BCT announced a more flexible exchange policy in 2012, as successive IMF programmes pushed for more flexibility, and since a 2016 circular it has used foreign currency auctions to make its interventions more transparent, Leaders reported at the time. The BCT still manages the rate; it is not a free float in the way the euro is.

Why the dinar is not freely convertible

Tunisia declared the dinar convertible for current transactions in 1992, but capital operations remain under the 1976 Foreign Exchange Code, which requires prior authorisation for many transactions. In practice, that has two consequences for anyone holding euros:

  • You cannot buy dinars outside Tunisia. Belgium's foreign ministry notes that the dinar "cannot be bought outside Tunisia".
  • Exporting dinar banknotes is prohibited, according to the same travel advice, updated in October 2026.

Reform of the code has been debated for years. One part has already passed: since 1 January 2026, residents can open foreign-currency accounts without prior BCT approval. Our article on the foreign exchange code reform tracks the rest.

What rate does the Central Bank of Tunisia publish?

Every day, the BCT publishes the previous day's average interbank rates on its website, for information. In a 2022 clarification reported by Kapitalis, the central bank stressed that this figure, although published on its site, "is not the BCT's rate" but the average of trades between banks, updated with a one-day lag.

This is the figure most people call the "official rate", and the one press reports and our live rate page use as a benchmark. It is a wholesale rate: no individual can trade at it.

Why do the official, bank, exchange office and transfer rates differ?

Each channel buys your euros below the interbank average and sells them above it. The gap is how it earns money.

  • Banks post a buying rate and a selling rate for each currency. The difference between them is the spread.
  • Exchange offices (bureaux de change), licensed by the BCT since 2019, may only buy and sell convertible currencies against dinars. Under BCT Circular 2018-07, they must display the rates they apply and issue an exchange slip (bordereau de change) for every purchase.
  • Money transfer providers set their own EUR/TND rate and often add a fee. In the World Bank's August 2025 survey of transfers from France, rate margins ranged from -0.46% to 7.31%, as detailed in our guide to the cheapest way to send money to Tunisia.

A "zero fee" offer is therefore not free if the rate is poor. The only fair comparison is the total amount of dinars received for the same number of euros.

What moved the euro against the dinar in 2025 and 2026?

The main driver has been the euro itself. In 2025, a stronger euro on world markets pushed EUR/TND up while the dinar gained against a weaker dollar. The BCT itself attributes day-to-day movements to "the variation of the euro and the dollar on the international market".

  • 2025: the euro started the year at 3.3209 dinars (31 December 2024, BCT). By 17 September 2025 it had reached 3.414, matching its July 2023 high, a 2.8% loss for the dinar since January, according to Webmanagercenter, citing TAP. An analyst quoted by TAP pointed to the euro's strength rather than domestic pressure. On 1 October 2025 the rate was 3.4247, La Presse reported.
  • End of 2025: the euro eased back to 3.3744 dinars on 31 December 2025. Over the year, the dinar lost 1.6% against the euro but gained 9.8% against the US dollar, according to the BCT's 2025 annual report cited by La Tribune, which links this to the dollar losing more than 12% against the euro.
  • 2026: at the end of June 2026 the dinar was stable against the euro compared with end-2025, while its average over the first half was 1.1% weaker than a year earlier, according to BCT data reported by La Presse. The BCT rate was 3.3731 on 29 June, 3.3724 on 29 September and 3.3687 on 5 October 2026.

Past movements do not predict future ones. Analysts quoted by La Presse also point to inflation, foreign currency reserves and international market swings as factors behind the dinar's value.

What are the currency rules for travellers and Tunisians abroad?

These rules are set by customs and BCT circulars and can change, so check with customs or your bank before travelling. As of 2026:

  • Declaration: you must declare foreign currency worth 20,000 dinars or more when entering or leaving Tunisia, under a 2016 finance ministry order amended in 2019. You should also declare smaller amounts if you plan to open a foreign-currency account or to take 5,000 dinars' worth or more back out. The declaration is personal, covers one trip and is valid for three months, according to Allo Justice, which echoes customs guidance.
  • Taking currency back out: non-residents may re-export up to 5,000 dinars' worth of foreign banknotes without an entry declaration, and up to 30,000 dinars' worth with one. Larger amounts must go through a bank.
  • Re-exchanging leftover dinars: non-residents who changed currency in Tunisia can convert remaining dinars back into foreign currency at a bank or licensed exchange office. A banking training guide based on BCT circulars says this requires the original exchange slip, plus the customs declaration for amounts of 5,000 dinars or more. Keep every bordereau de change.
  • On the European side: leaving France, or any EU country, with €10,000 or more in cash must be declared to customs.

Tunisians living abroad are generally non-residents for exchange purposes, but they can be treated as residents for some operations in Tunisia, such as buying property. Our guide to receiving money in Tunisia covers convertible-dinar and foreign-currency accounts.

Is changing money on the black market worth it?

Informal changers operate in Tunisia, notably in Ben Guerdane and near the Libyan and Algerian borders, and the BCT's daily dashboard tracks parallel rates there, African Manager reported in 2024. They may offer a few more dinars per euro, but the risks outweigh the gain:

  • foreign exchange outside authorised intermediaries breaches the exchange code;
  • you get no exchange slip, so you cannot legally re-exchange leftover dinars;
  • counterfeit notes, short-changing and theft leave you with no recourse.

Belgium's travel advice "strongly recommends" not changing money in the street.

How can I get the best euro to dinar rate?

  1. Check the benchmark first. Compare any quote with the BCT's interbank average to see the margin you are paying.
  2. Compare the total received, not the fee or the headline rate. Our money transfer comparator lists offers for sending euros to Tunisia.
  3. Prefer online transfers to cash at a counter, where the World Bank found the widest margins.
  4. Avoid changing at the airport out of habit. Look at the posted buy rate at banks, La Poste and licensed bureaux in town.
  5. Use formal channels and keep receipts. Informal exchange saves little and costs you legal protection. Formal transfers also support Tunisia's foreign currency earnings, as we explain in our article on diaspora remittances.

Frequently asked questions

What is the official euro to Tunisian dinar exchange rate?

The figure usually called the official rate is the average interbank rate that the Central Bank of Tunisia publishes each day for the previous day. The BCT says it is not its own rate but the average of trades between banks. Individuals change money at bank, exchange office or provider rates, which include a margin.

Can I buy Tunisian dinars before I travel?

No. The dinar cannot be bought outside Tunisia, and exporting dinar banknotes is prohibited. Bring euros and change them on arrival at a bank, La Poste or a licensed exchange office, or withdraw dinars from an ATM in Tunisia.

How much money can I bring into Tunisia without declaring it?

Foreign currency worth 20,000 dinars or more must be declared to customs when entering or leaving Tunisia. You should also declare smaller amounts if you plan to open a foreign-currency account or take 5,000 dinars' worth or more back out. Rules can change, so check with customs before travelling.

Can I change my leftover dinars back into euros?

Non-residents can re-exchange leftover dinars at a bank or licensed exchange office, provided they show the exchange slip from the original transaction, plus a customs declaration for 5,000 dinars or more. Keep every bordereau de change until you leave.

Why is the euro to dinar rate different at my bank?

Banks buy euros below the interbank average and sell them above it, and transfer providers add their own margin and sometimes a fee. Compare how many dinars you receive in total for the same amount of euros.
