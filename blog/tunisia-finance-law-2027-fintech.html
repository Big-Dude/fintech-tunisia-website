Tunisia Finance Law 2027: What the Draft Means for Fintech

The PLF 2027 is light on fintech but has concrete measures: zero duty on payment terminals, seller reporting for delivery firms and small startup credit lines.

Tunisia's draft Finance Law 2027 went to parliament on 15 October 2026, and its measures for digital payments are few but concrete. The bill would scrap customs duties on payment terminals, cut duties on smartphones, make delivery companies report online sellers to the tax office, and open small credit lines for labelled startups. It says nothing new on foreign exchange or cash limits. Everything below is a draft until parliament votes, which must happen by 10 December 2026.

What is in Tunisia's draft Finance Law 2027?

The projet de loi de finances (PLF) 2027 sets state spending at about TND 68.9 billion, up 8.37% on the 2026 Finance Law, according to figures obtained by TAP and reported by Tunisie-Tribune. Revenue is projected at TND 54.28 billion, including about TND 49.6 billion in taxes. The deficit stands at TND 14.6 billion and total Treasury financing needs at TND 28.1 billion, of which TND 19.8 billion would be borrowed domestically and TND 6.9 billion abroad.

For the financial sector, the biggest line is not a fintech measure. The draft again lets the Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT) lend the Treasury up to TND 9.8 billion, interest-free and repayable over 15 years with three years' grace, by derogation from the BCT statute (Law 2016-35), Business News reported. The draft 2026 budget had provided for up to TND 11 billion on the same terms. Direct central bank financing means the state leans less on bank credit, but it remains a point of concern for economists worried about inflation and the dinar.

How does the PLF 2027 treat payment terminals and smartphones?

Three articles touch payment hardware, as reported by TAP, La Presse and Le Temps:

  • Article 69 would set customs duty on imported electronic payment terminals (TPE, or POS terminals) at 0% and add them to Table A of the VAT Code, the list of exempt operations. The stated aim is to make electronic payment easier and support digital transition.
  • Article 68 would cut customs duty on imported smartphones to 10%. Press reports did not give the current rate.
  • Article 67 would charge a TND 20 stamp duty on each registration of a phone in the central equipment identifier registry, with the first registration exempt. Declaration of the duty would be handled by the telecoms research centre CERT.

For merchants, a cheaper terminal removes one small barrier to accepting cards and QR payments. It does not change the bigger obstacles: commission fees, low card ownership and the habits described in our piece on Tunisia's cash economy, where notes and coins in circulation topped TND 30 billion in 2026. The smartphone duty cut helps wallet adoption at the margin; the new TND 20 fee on re-registration partly offsets it for people who buy second-hand or imported phones.

What changes for e-commerce and cash on delivery?

Article 66 is the measure most likely to affect online sellers. According to Business News, it would require companies delivering goods sold online or through audiovisual media, and platforms connecting sellers with couriers, to file a list of their suppliers every six months. Each entry would include the seller's identity, tax number or, failing that, national ID card number, and transaction amounts.

The penalties: a fine of 5% of undeclared transactions, plus TND 200 for each missing, incomplete or wrong item. African Manager noted that the measure creates no new tax, targets goods rather than online services, and that, if it applies from 1 January 2027, the first list would be due by mid-July 2027.

This matters because much of Tunisian social-media commerce runs on cash on delivery, and the courier is often the only party who sees both seller and payment (see e-commerce payments in Tunisia). The government presents Article 66 as a way to fight informality. Critics, including African Manager, warn that sellers could switch to self-delivery or undeclared couriers, and ask how mass collection of ID numbers fits the 2004 data protection law.

What does the draft offer startups?

The startup measures are modest in size, and all would be managed by public banks, according to La Presse:

  • Article 13: TND 10 million in interest-free participatory loans for founders of companies holding the Startup label, to strengthen equity, from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2028, managed by BFPME and funded by the National Employment Fund.
  • Article 14: the state would cover employer social security contributions, on the minimum wage basis, for small businesses created in 2027 (100%, then 75%, then 50% over three years), capped at TND 5 million in total; plus a TND 10 million working-capital line for young small firms, managed by BTS.
  • Article 16: TND 5 million in preferential loans for holders of the student-entrepreneur status.
  • Article 22: companies obtaining an investment declaration receipt between 2027 and 2030 would be exempt from corporate tax under the conditions of the 2024 Finance Law. Financial activities are excluded, as are telecom operators, so most fintechs would not qualify.

TND 10 million for startup equity is small next to typical seed rounds, and the eligibility rules will be set later by agreements between ministries.

Is there anything on banks, foreign exchange or the diaspora?

Very little. Press coverage of the draft reports no new levy on banks, no change to cash payment rules and no foreign exchange liberalisation. The FX code reform is a separate bill still awaiting the BCT's opinion, and resident foreign-currency accounts were already opened by the 2026 Finance Law.

Two items touch the diaspora indirectly. The travel tax for trips abroad would rise from TND 60 to TND 80 (outside Maghreb countries, according to Réalités), to fund the social security diversification account. And in committee hearings, MPs raised the difficulties of Tunisians abroad with cash-only consular payments and access to international cards; the foreign minister said remittances had reached TND 7.5 billion by October.

On e-invoicing, Finance Committee chair Maher Ketari said in early October that a revision of the phased rollout would be proposed. We could not confirm that it appears in the government's text, so treat it as a possible amendment.

When will the Finance Law 2027 be voted?

Committees of both chambers have been examining the budget since mid-October. Ketari said party groups agreed to limit amendments to four additional articles per group, after more than 140 were tabled last year. According to Espace Manager, joint plenary sessions of the ARP and the National Council of Regions and Districts open on 9 November, ministry budgets are debated until about 25 November, and the Finance Law itself follows. The Constitution requires adoption by 10 December 2026.

What should fintech founders watch?

  • Final wording of Article 66: whether "transaction amounts" means the goods' price or just delivery fees, and whether small occasional sellers are exempted.
  • Amendments: MPs could add measures on e-invoicing, cash or digital payments in late November.
  • Implementing texts: the startup loans need ministerial agreements before money moves.
  • Terminal prices: whether acquirers and banks pass the duty cut on to merchants.

We will update this article once parliament votes.

Frequently asked questions

When will Tunisia's Finance Law 2027 be adopted?

The draft was sent to parliament on 15 October 2026. Joint plenary sessions of the ARP and the National Council of Regions and Districts start on 9 November, and the Constitution requires the Finance Law to be adopted by 10 December 2026.

Does the PLF 2027 remove customs duties on payment terminals?

Yes, as drafted. Article 69 would set customs duty on imported electronic payment terminals at 0% and add them to Table A of the VAT Code. The measure still has to survive the parliamentary vote.

What does Article 66 of the PLF 2027 mean for online sellers?

Delivery companies and platforms linking sellers with couriers would have to send the tax administration, every six months, a list of sellers with their tax number or ID card number and transaction amounts. Missing declarations would cost 5% of the undeclared transactions plus TND 200 per missing or wrong item.

Is there anything for startups in Tunisia's 2027 budget?

The draft includes TND 10 million in interest-free participatory loans for labelled startups in 2027–2028, social security relief for small firms created in 2027 and TND 5 million for student-entrepreneurs. A corporate tax exemption for new companies excludes financial activities.

Does the Finance Law 2027 change foreign exchange rules?

Not according to press coverage of the draft. The new FX code is a separate bill still in committee, and resident foreign-currency accounts were already allowed by the 2026 Finance Law.
