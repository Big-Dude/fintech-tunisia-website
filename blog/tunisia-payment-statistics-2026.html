Tunisia Payment Statistics 2026: Cards, Mobile and E-commerce

The Central Bank's latest payments bulletin shows online card payments up 27%, mobile wallets up 53% and cheques still falling. Here is what the numbers say, and what they don't.

The Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT) published issue 18 of its bulletin Les paiements en chiffres on 10 November 2026, covering 1 January to 30 September. Every electronic channel grew faster than a year earlier, and the cheque kept shrinking. But cash is still how most Tunisians use their cards. This article sums up the figures, as reported by Challenges, Managers, L'Économiste Maghrébin and Webdo. Year-on-year changes compare with the first nine months of 2025.

How many card payments are made in Tunisia?

Card operations ("monétique", which includes withdrawals) reached 135.7 million, up 10.5%, for a value of TND 24.4 billion, up 11.1%. Payments made up 44% of those operations, against 40% a year earlier, and 26% of their value.

In other words, 56% of card operations were still ATM withdrawals. The card is gaining ground as a way to pay, but it remains mostly a way to get cash. There were about 6.49 million cards in circulation, 3,333 ATMs and 45,900 payment terminals (TPE) at the end of September.

Indicator (Jan.–Sept. 2026) Volume Value Change in number
All card operations 135.7 million TND 24.43bn +10.5%
In-store card payments (TPE) 41.3 million TND 5.14bn +18.6%
Online card payments 18.0 million TND 1.23bn +27.0%
Mobile payment operations 7.1 million TND 1.35bn +20%

How fast is e-commerce growing in Tunisia?

Online card payments were the fastest-growing channel: 18 million transactions (+27%) worth TND 1.23 billion (+24.1%). The number of active merchant websites, those with at least one payment in the past 12 months, rose 27.6% to 1,552.

Challenges worked out an average online basket of about TND 68, against about TND 125 for an in-store card payment. That fits the profile of Tunisian e-payment: bills, telecom top-ups, government services and school fees as much as online shopping. Cash on delivery, which the bulletin doesn't measure, remains the norm for e-commerce orders. Our guide to e-commerce payments explains why.

In shops, card payments rose 18.6% to 41.3 million, for TND 5.14 billion. With only 45,900 terminals for the whole country, acceptance is still the bottleneck. That is why the draft Finance Law 2027 proposes cutting customs duty on payment terminals.

How many people use mobile payment in Tunisia?

The BCT counted 719,000 mobile wallets at the end of September, up 53.3%, run by 20 payment service providers. They handled 7.1 million operations (+20%) worth TND 1.35 billion (+6.6%).

By number, 64.2% of those operations were payments, 20.7% transfers between people, 10.4% cash deposits (cash-in) and 4.7% withdrawals (cash-out). By value, payments accounted for 56%.

Two cautions apply. First, the previous bulletin counted 512,000 wallets at the end of June, so the stock grew by about 40% in a single quarter. That suggests account openings are outpacing use, a pattern the BCT's 2025 supervision report had already described: only 11.3% of payment accounts at payment institutions were active at the end of 2025. Second, value is growing much more slowly than volume, so the average mobile transaction is getting smaller. Webdo's projection of about 10 million mobile transactions for 2026 is its own estimate, not a BCT forecast.

For background on the players, see our guides to mobile payments in Tunisia and payment institutions.

Is the cheque disappearing in Tunisia?

Slowly. Cheques were the only instrument in decline: 5.5 million cleared (-7.6%) for TND 37.3 billion (-7.3%). They still carry about 22% of the value cleared through the interbank clearing system, which handled 45.5 million operations worth TND 168.5 billion (+4.7%).

Clearing (Jan.–Sept. 2026) Number Change Value Rejection rate (number)
Credit transfers 29.5 million +5.0% TND 62.8bn 0.1%
Direct debits 6.8 million +13.8% TND 25.4bn 49.3%
Cheques 5.5 million -7.6% TND 37.3bn 0.9%
Bills of exchange 3.6 million +8.7% TND 43.1bn 9.4%

Since the cheque law reform took effect in February 2025, with a new cheque format and the TuniChèque platform for checking funds in real time, many businesses have moved to bills of exchange (lettres de change), up 8.7%. Direct debits grew 13.8%, but almost half of them (49.3%) were rejected, usually for lack of funds. That is a warning for any fintech planning to collect subscriptions or loan repayments by debit.

What is TunPay?

TunPay is the national label for mobile payment, created by a BCT note to banks (No. 2026-132) on 5 May 2026 and developed with the Société Monétique Tunisie (SMT). Wallet issuers (banks, La Poste and payment institutions) are expected to display it on their apps and at acceptance points so customers can recognise where they can pay by phone. It is a visual identity, not a new payment network.

The bulletin lists the adoption of TunPay among completed projects, alongside paying school fees by USSD or wallet. Projects still in progress include the overhaul of interbank clearing, the restructuring of SMT and SIBTEL, and the White-EMV card scheme.

What the numbers mean for fintechs

  • The demand is there. E-commerce and wallet volumes are growing at 20–27% a year, faster than banking as a whole.
  • Use per account is weak. Wallets are multiplying faster than transactions, so activation and everyday use cases matter more than sign-ups.
  • Acceptance is the constraint. One terminal for about 140 cards limits in-store growth, which helps QR and phone-based acceptance.
  • Rules are tightening. From late December, payment institutions must comply with Circular 2026-10, with new governance, security and partnership rules.

The BCT publishes Les paiements en chiffres each quarter. We will update these figures when issue 19, covering the full year 2026, is out.

Frequently asked questions

How many mobile wallets are there in Tunisia?

The Central Bank of Tunisia counted 719,000 mobile wallets at the end of September 2026, up 53.3% in a year, run by 20 payment service providers. They handled 7.1 million operations worth TND 1.35 billion in the first nine months of 2026.

How big is e-commerce in Tunisia?

Online card payments reached 18 million transactions worth about TND 1.23 billion in January–September 2026, up 27% in number. There were 1,552 active merchant websites. Cash on delivery is not included in these figures.

Do Tunisians still use cash?

Yes. 56% of card operations in the first nine months of 2026 were cash withdrawals, and most everyday purchases are still paid in cash, although card and mobile payments are growing by 18–27% a year.

Where do these figures come from?

From the Central Bank of Tunisia's quarterly bulletin Les paiements en chiffres en Tunisie, issue 18, published on 10 November 2026 and covering data to 30 September 2026, as reported by the Tunisian business press.
