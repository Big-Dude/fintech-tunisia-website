Egypt Fintech: InstaPay, MNT-Halan and Fawry Explained

A central-bank payment rail, a listed incumbent and a unicorn lender: how Egypt built North Africa's biggest fintech market, and what Tunisia can take from it.

Egypt has become North Africa's largest fintech market, built on three very different engines: a central-bank payment rail, a listed payments incumbent and a venture-backed lender. Egypt fintech is now a case study in how public infrastructure and private companies can grow each other. For Tunisian founders, regulators and bankers, the Egyptian model offers practical lessons, as well as a few warnings.

InstaPay: the public rail behind Egypt fintech growth

The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) launched its Instant Payment Network, and the InstaPay app that sits on top of it, in April 2022. InstaPay links all of Egypt's banks in one system and lets their customers send money instantly, 24 hours a day. Recent updates let users share a QR code or payment link that fills in the beneficiary's details.

Adoption was fast. According to Egyptian Streets, InstaPay recorded 2.16 million users, 20.3 million transactions and EGP 112.7 billion in value in its first year, and its transactions in 2024 were worth EGP 2.9 trillion. In October 2026, CBE Governor Hassan Abdalla said the network had reached around 22 million registered customers, with 1.47 billion transactions worth about EGP 4.33 trillion by the end of the second quarter of 2026, as reported by Egypt Today.

From free to paid

InstaPay was free at launch. Since 1 April 2025, it charges 0.1% of each transfer, with a minimum of EGP 0.50 and a maximum of EGP 20. Users get ten free balance inquiries a month. Transfers are capped at EGP 70,000 per transaction and EGP 400,000 per month.

A senior CBE official, cited by EnterpriseAM and reported by Zawya, said that InstaPay became profitable after the fee was introduced and that usage kept growing. A small, capped fee did not slow adoption; it paid for the infrastructure.

Fawry: the incumbent that became a lender

Fawry started in 2008 as an electronic bill payment network linking utilities and telecom operators with consumers through retail agents. It is now one of Egypt's most profitable fintechs. Its FY2025 results show:

  • revenue of EGP 8.65 billion, up 57%;
  • net profit after minority interests of EGP 2.89 billion, up 79.8%;
  • throughput of EGP 943.6 billion;
  • 54.8 million active network customers and about 376,600 POS terminals.

The biggest change is in the revenue mix. Financial services, which include consumer finance (buy now, pay later) and loans to small businesses, grew 135% to EGP 2.38 billion and now make up 27.5% of revenue. Banking services, which include acceptance and agent banking, remain the largest segment. The agent network that once collected utility bills now distributes credit.

MNT-Halan: Egypt's fintech unicorn

MNT-Halan, founded by Mounir Nakhla, became Egypt's first fintech unicorn in 2023. In June 2026, it reached a $1.4 billion valuation after the first close of a round led by Al Ahly Capital, the investment arm of the state-owned National Bank of Egypt, according to Wamda. The amount was not disclosed.

The group combines consumer and business lending, payments, e-wallets, savings, investment and e-commerce. It operates in Egypt and Türkiye, owns a microfinance bank in Pakistan and entered the UAE in 2024. Most of the new money is earmarked for Egypt.

What made the Egyptian model work

Several features stand out:

  • A central bank that builds. The CBE did not just regulate payments; it launched a national instant payment network and app that link every bank.
  • Pricing that pays for the rail. InstaPay moved from free to a small, capped fee once habits were in place.
  • Payments as a route to credit. Both Fawry and MNT-Halan use payment data and distribution networks to lend.
  • Domestic capital. A state bank's investment arm led MNT-Halan's round.

There are risks too. InstaPay transfers are irreversible, which puts the burden of fraud prevention on users and banks. Rapid lending growth through fintech channels also needs close supervision of credit quality.

Lessons for Tunisia

The difference in scale is large. Tunisia recorded 8.4 million mobile payment transactions in 2025, worth 1.769 billion dinars, according to Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT) data reported by Ecofin Agency. Egypt's population is much larger, but the gap is also about infrastructure.

For Tunisia, the useful lessons are:

  1. Build a shared rail with a simple user experience. Instant transfers with a QR code or payment link work when every bank is connected. Our article on instant payments and interoperability in Tunisia looks at where the country stands.
  2. Charge a little, transparently. A capped fee can make a public rail self-financing without driving users back to cash.
  3. Let payment companies grow into credit, under supervision. Fawry's shift shows where the margins are. In Tunisia, this touches the microfinance framework and the payment institution regime.
  4. Mobilise local institutional capital. Al Ahly Capital's role is a reminder that banks and public funds can back fintechs. Tunisia has its own tools, such as the ANAVA fund of funds.

Frequently asked questions

How much does InstaPay charge per transfer?

Since 1 April 2025, InstaPay charges 0.1% of the amount transferred, with a minimum of EGP 0.50 and a maximum of EGP 20 per transaction. Ten balance inquiries per month are free, then each costs EGP 0.50.

What is the InstaPay transfer limit?

Transfers are capped at EGP 70,000 per transaction and EGP 400,000 per month, according to reporting in 2025. Limits can change, so users should check with their bank.

Is MNT-Halan a unicorn?

Yes. MNT-Halan became Egypt's first fintech unicorn in 2023 and reached a $1.4 billion valuation in June 2026 after a round led by Al Ahly Capital.

Does Tunisia have an equivalent of InstaPay?

Tunisia does not have a national app at InstaPay's scale. In 2026 the Central Bank of Tunisia and Monétique Tunisie introduced the TUNPAY label to unify mobile payments and improve interoperability.
