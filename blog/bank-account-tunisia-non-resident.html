Opening a Bank Account in Tunisia from Abroad: 2026 Guide for the Diaspora

Convertible dinars, foreign currency or ordinary dinars? What each account lets Tunisians abroad and foreigners do, which documents banks ask for, who opens accounts online, and how to make sure you can transfer your money back.

Key takeaways

  • Non-residents, including Tunisians living abroad for more than two years, can open three kinds of account: ordinary dinars, convertible dinars and foreign currency.
  • Convertible-dinar and foreign-currency accounts are the ones you can transfer back abroad; money on an ordinary dinar account generally needs Central Bank authorisation to leave Tunisia.

  • Keep proof of every foreign-currency transfer or customs declaration: it is what lets you repatriate the money, including the proceeds of a property sale.

More than a million Tunisians live abroad, and many keep a bank account at home to support family, save, buy property or prepare a return. Foreigners working with Tunisia need one too. But Tunisia's foreign exchange rules, based on the 1976 exchange code, make the type of account you open matter a lot: it decides whether you can send the money back. This guide explains the options. It is based on the pages of Attijari bank and Banque de Tunisie and on 2026 guides by Les Afriques and CFR Audit. Rules and fees vary by bank: confirm with yours before you transfer money.

Are you a resident or a non-resident?

Everything depends on your exchange-control status, not your nationality. A Tunisian who has lived and worked abroad for more than two years is generally treated as a non-resident. Attijari, for example, offers Tunisians abroad for more than two years a dinar, convertible-dinar and foreign-currency account, and those abroad for less than two years a dinar account plus a convertible-dinar account. Foreigners living outside Tunisia are non-residents too.

Which account should you open?

Account Currency Can you transfer the money abroad? Typical use
Convertible-dinar account (compte en dinars convertibles) Dinars, freely convertible Yes, funds came from abroad Spending and saving in Tunisia while keeping the right to repatriate
Foreign-currency account (compte en devises) EUR, USD, GBP and others Yes Holding euros or dollars without exchange risk
Ordinary dinar account Dinars Generally only with Central Bank (BCT) authorisation Local bills, family support, salary from Tunisia

The convertible-dinar account is the workhorse for the diaspora. It is credited with money you bring from abroad (transfers, or declared foreign banknotes), converted to dinars, and you can use it in Tunisia or transfer it back abroad. It cannot be overdrawn. According to Banque de Tunisie, interest on a convertible-dinar term deposit is fully tax-exempt, while a dinar term deposit has 20% withheld.

The foreign-currency account keeps your money in euros, dollars or another currency, so you don't lose if the dinar moves (see the euro to dinar rate today).

The ordinary dinar account is useful for everyday spending in Tunisia, but money on it, for example rent from a Tunisian property or an inheritance, generally can't be transferred abroad without BCT authorisation.

Since 1 January 2026, the Finance Law also lets residents open foreign-currency or convertible-dinar accounts without prior BCT authorisation, which helps people who move back.

What documents do you need?

Banks' lists differ, but expect:

  • a valid passport (and for Tunisians, the national ID card or consular card);
  • proof of residence abroad: residence permit, foreign ID or a recent proof of address;
  • sometimes proof of income or work abroad (employment contract, payslips);
  • for an initial deposit in foreign banknotes, the customs declaration stamped on arrival. Foreign cash of TND 20,000 or more must be declared when you enter Tunisia.

Can you open an account online from abroad?

Increasingly, yes. Attijari bank advertises 100% online account opening for Tunisians abroad. Other banks, including BIAT, Amen Bank and BNA, offer remote or partly digital onboarding or representatives in Europe, according to the 2026 guides; some still ask for documents certified at a consulate or a visit to a branch on your next trip. Ask about timing: guides report anything from a few days in a branch to one to three weeks remotely.

How do you get your money back out of Tunisia?

The rule of thumb: money that came in as foreign currency, with proof, can go back out.

  • Savings: funds on a convertible-dinar or foreign-currency account can be transferred abroad.
  • Property bought with foreign currency: the sale price can be transferred back with the supporting documents. Declaring the investment through the BCT's investment form gives a transfer guarantee.
  • Property bought in dinars or inherited: there is no automatic transfer right; you need BCT authorisation, which CFR Audit describes as hard to obtain. The proceeds usually go to a restricted account.
  • Cash: non-residents can take out foreign currency they declared on arrival; keep exchange slips and declarations.

A reform of the exchange code (bill No. 115/2025) would loosen these rules, but it was still before Parliament's finance committee in autumn 2026. See our foreign exchange code reform tracker.

Tips before you choose a bank

  • Open a convertible-dinar or foreign-currency account first, not just a dinar account, if you may ever want to repatriate.
  • Send money by bank transfer rather than carrying cash, and keep every transfer receipt. To find the cheapest way, use our transfer comparator and our guide to the cheapest way to send money to Tunisia.
  • Compare fees for account keeping, cards and incoming transfers; they differ between banks.
  • Check digital banking. Managing the account from abroad is much easier with a good app; see our review of digital banking in Tunisia.

Frequently asked questions

Can I open a Tunisian bank account from abroad?

Yes. Several banks offer remote opening for Tunisians abroad; Attijari bank advertises fully online opening. Others accept documents certified at a consulate or through representatives in Europe, and some require a visit to a branch.

What is a convertible-dinar account?

A dinar account for non-residents funded with money brought from abroad. The balance stays convertible: you can spend it in Tunisia or transfer it back abroad. Interest on convertible-dinar term deposits is exempt from income tax, according to Banque de Tunisie.

Can I transfer money from my Tunisian dinar account abroad?

Generally not without Central Bank authorisation. Only funds on convertible-dinar or foreign-currency accounts, or proceeds of investments made with declared foreign currency, can be transferred back freely with supporting documents.

Who counts as a non-resident in Tunisia?

Foreigners living abroad and Tunisians who have lived and worked abroad for more than two years are generally treated as non-residents for exchange-control purposes.
