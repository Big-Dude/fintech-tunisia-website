Tunisia Incubators and Accelerators for Fintech Founders

From Flat6Labs' seed tickets to bank-run labs and Visa's African fintech accelerator: where Tunisian fintech founders can find support, and how to choose.

Tunisia has no shortage of incubators and accelerators, but few are built specifically for fintech. A founder building a payments or lending product has to combine general startup programmes, bank-run labs, pan-African fintech accelerators and the central bank's own sandbox. This guide sets out the main options as of October 2026, what each offers, and how to choose.

The landscape at a glance

Support structures in Tunisia fall into five groups:

  • Investor-led accelerators that put money in, such as Flat6Labs Tunis;
  • Bank-run programmes, such as BIATLABS and open-innovation programmes run by other banks;
  • Corporate accelerators, such as Orange Fab Tunisie;
  • Hubs and incubators, such as The Dot and Wiki Start Up;
  • Public schemes: the Startup Act label, the ANAVA fund of funds and, for regulated products, the BCT regulatory sandbox.

Briter Bridges counted 29 tech hubs in Tunisia back in 2020, mostly in Tunis, according to a GSMA analysis. Smart Capital said at the time that 43% of Tunisian startups received support from a hub.

Flat6Labs Tunis: the most active seed investor

Flat6Labs Tunis was set up in 2017 with backing from Le15, Meninx Holding, TAEF and BIAT. According to Launch Base Africa (May 2024), its seed programme offers:

  • an initial investment of up to TND 200,000;
  • possible follow-on funding of TND 200,000 to 800,000;
  • a four-month programme with mentoring, office space and partner perks.

In May 2024, Launch Base Africa reported the launch of Flat6Labs' Tunisia Seed Fund II, with Smart Capital as a partner. The same report describes the Anava Seed Fund, a TND 30 million vehicle backed by IFC, BIAT, TAEF, Meninx Holding and Sawari Ventures, designed to invest in up to 90 early-stage startups over five years. Flat6Labs is also one of the venture managers backed by the ANAVA fund of funds. It runs non-equity programmes too: with UNDP Tunisia it opened applications in September 2026 for the sixth cohort of the Green Growth and Jobs Accelerator. That five-month programme supports 25 SMEs and startups without direct funding.

Bank-run programmes: closest to fintech

Banks are the natural partners for most Tunisian fintechs, and some run their own programmes:

  • BIATLABS (B@labs) was launched by BIAT in 2017 and describes itself as the first incubator set up by a Tunisian bank. At launch it named fintech and AI as priority sectors. In 2021 it added a three-month pre-incubation programme for founders at the idea stage.
  • Attijari Bank announced CorpUP Fintech in December 2021, run with Portugal's Beta-i and Tunisian venture studio Betacube. The five-month programme matched startups that already had a tested product with the bank to build pilots in payments, digital signatures and customer connectivity.

For a fintech, the main value of these programmes is often access to the bank itself: its compliance team, its IT systems and a possible first enterprise customer.

Orange Fab and corporate programmes

Orange Fab Tunisie, the accelerator of Orange Tunisie, started in April 2019. Its eighth cohort, announced in March 2026, focused on AI and brought the total number of accelerated startups to 45. It is part of the Orange Digital Center in Les Berges du Lac. According to the French Treasury's economic service, the centre had supported 162 startups and also runs a coding school, a FabLab and a 5G lab. Orange Fab is not fintech-specific, but it opens doors to a telecom operator, which matters for anyone building mobile payments.

Hubs and incubators

  • The Dot, in Les Berges du Lac, came out of a 2018 French-Tunisian initiative. Smart Tunisia is the lead partner, with support from Expertise France and GIZ. It offers a 2,900 m² space, programmes from pre-incubation to financing, and The Dot Academy for tech skills.
  • Wiki Start Up, founded in 2011 by Mondher Khanfir and Mehdi Khemiri, was described by Wamda at the time as Tunisia's first startup incubator. It combined advisory services with a pre-seed fund.

Pan-African fintech accelerators

Specialised fintech programmes are mostly regional. The Visa Africa Fintech Accelerator was launched in 2023 as part of Visa's pledge to invest $1 billion in financial inclusion in Africa by 2027. By early 2025 it had supported 64 startups across three cohorts and was recruiting a fourth. Visa has also invested in Tunisian payments company Konnect Networks. Check each call for applications for eligibility, since country lists change between cohorts.

How to choose a programme

  • Funding or no funding? Investor-led programmes take equity. Programmes run by development agencies or corporates often give mentoring and visibility but no cash.
  • Regulatory help. For a product that holds customer funds, a bank or telecom partner and the BCT sandbox matter more than general coaching.
  • Follow-on capital. Ask which funds invest after the programme. In Tunisia, most early-stage money traces back to ANAVA.
  • Track record. Ask for alumni contacts and talk to founders from earlier cohorts before you sign anything.

Accelerators can shorten the path to a first customer or a first cheque, but they do not replace a licence strategy. For the fundraising side, see our guide to raising money as a Tunisian fintech.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best accelerators for fintech startups in Tunisia?

There is no fintech-only national accelerator. Founders typically look at Flat6Labs Tunis for seed investment, bank programmes such as BIATLABS, Orange Fab for telecom access, pan-African programmes such as the Visa Africa Fintech Accelerator, and the BCT regulatory sandbox for licensing.

How much does Flat6Labs invest in Tunisian startups?

According to Launch Base Africa (2024), Flat6Labs Tunis offers an initial investment of up to TND 200,000, with potential follow-on funding of TND 200,000 to 800,000, alongside a four-month programme.

Do Tunisian accelerators help with BCT licensing?

Not directly. Accelerators can introduce founders to banks and investors, but licensing goes through the Central Bank of Tunisia, often via its regulatory sandbox or a partnership with a licensed institution.
