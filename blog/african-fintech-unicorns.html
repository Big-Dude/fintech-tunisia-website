Africa's Fintech Unicorns: Moniepoint, OPay, Wave and More

Almost every African unicorn is a fintech. Who they are, how reliable their valuations are, and why none of them is Tunisian.

Africa has only a handful of startups valued at $1 billion or more, and almost all of them are fintechs. Africa's fintech unicorns are concentrated in Nigeria, with outliers in Egypt, Senegal and South Africa. Their stories show what kind of business reaches scale on the continent, and why valuations should be read with care.

Who are Africa's fintech unicorns?

Lists vary because companies rarely publish valuations. Afridigest, last updated in September 2025, counts nine unicorns, eight of them fintechs: Interswitch, Flutterwave, OPay, Wave, Chipper Cash, MNT-Halan, Moniepoint and Tyme. The ninth is Andela, a tech talent company. BusinessDay reported in August 2026 that Nigeria's Moove, a vehicle-financing company, had joined the club, taking the total to ten.

Flutterwave (Nigeria)

The payments company, founded in 2016, is Africa's most valuable fintech. TechCabal reported that its June 2026 Series E, backed by Ripple, valued it at $3.25 billion. Its strategy is covered in our piece on Flutterwave, Paystack and Africa's payment rails.

OPay (Nigeria)

OPay's last external valuation was $2 billion, after a $400 million Series C led by SoftBank's Vision Fund 2 in 2021. According to Launch Base Africa, Opera's accounts value its 9.5% stake at $294.6 million, which implies about $3.1 billion for the whole company. Opera's model assumes an 85% chance of a stock market listing within two years. OPay has more than 60 million users in Nigeria, according to figures cited in the same article.

Moniepoint (Nigeria)

Moniepoint, founded in 2015, built its business on payments and business accounts for small merchants. It passed $1 billion in valuation in 2024. In October 2025, Launch Base Africa reported that it had completed a $200 million round with Visa, Development Partners International, LeapFrog and Google's Africa fund. It processes more than one billion transactions and over $22 billion a month, and has bought licensed entities in the UK and Kenya.

Wave (Senegal)

Wave reached a $1.7 billion valuation with a $200 million Series A in 2021, after cutting mobile money fees in Senegal. That valuation has not been updated publicly since. Its later financing, including a $137 million debt facility in 2025, did not come with a new valuation. See how Wave disrupted mobile money pricing.

MNT-Halan (Egypt)

Egypt's first fintech unicorn combines lending, payments, wallets and e-commerce. In June 2026, Wamda reported a $1.4 billion valuation after a round led by Al Ahly Capital, the investment arm of the National Bank of Egypt. It also operates in Türkiye, Pakistan and the UAE. More on the Egyptian market in our article on Egypt's fintech boom.

Tyme Group (South Africa)

The digital bank behind TymeBank in South Africa and GoTyme in the Philippines became a unicorn in December 2024, when Brazil's Nubank led a $250 million round at a $1.5 billion post-money valuation, according to GoTyme Bank. Tyme reported more than 15 million customers at the time.

Interswitch and Chipper Cash

Interswitch, Nigeria's payment switching company, reached a $1 billion valuation with a Visa investment in 2019. Chipper Cash was valued at slightly over $2 billion in 2021 but has not raised since, and has made several rounds of layoffs, according to Africa: The Big Deal.

Paper valuations, real caution

A unicorn label says when a company was last priced, not what it is worth today. Several African valuations date from the 2021 funding boom. Africa: The Big Deal noted in 2024 that Chipper Cash's valuation may now be lower, and that Fawry, often called a unicorn after its 2019 listing in Cairo, was then worth well under $1 billion on the stock market. OPay's $3.1 billion is an accounting estimate by one shareholder, not a funding round.

The same caution applies to unconfirmed claims. BusinessDay excluded Algeria's Yassir from its list because the company has never officially disclosed a valuation above $1 billion.

What they have in common

The fintech unicorns share several traits:

  • Payments as the entry point. Almost all started with transfers, merchant payments or card switching.
  • Lending and deposits for revenue. MNT-Halan, Moniepoint and Tyme earn much of their money from credit and banking, which requires licences.
  • A large home market. Nigeria and Egypt are among Africa's most populous countries, giving room to scale before going abroad.
  • Physical distribution. OPay, Moniepoint and Wave built agent or merchant networks, not just apps.
  • Licences as assets. Moniepoint bought licensed entities in the UK and Kenya; Paystack and others have also bought banks.

What it means for Tunisia

Tunisia has no unicorn. Its best-known exit, InstaDeep's acquisition by BioNTech in 2023, was in artificial intelligence, not fintech (see lessons from InstaDeep). A small domestic market, foreign exchange rules and a still-modest digital payments base make a Tunisian fintech unicorn hard to build from the home market alone.

For Tunisian founders, the lessons are practical:

  • Plan for a bigger market early, whether North Africa, francophone West Africa or the diaspora in Europe.
  • Treat licensing as strategy. The unicorns that grew fastest used banking or payment licences to move into lending and deposits.
  • Build distribution, not just software. Agents, merchants and partnerships with La Poste or telecom operators matter as much as the app.
  • Read valuations critically. A headline number from 2021 says little about what investors will pay in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

How many fintech unicorns are there in Africa?

Afridigest's list, last updated in September 2025, counts nine African unicorns, eight of them fintechs: Interswitch, Flutterwave, OPay, Wave, Chipper Cash, MNT-Halan, Moniepoint and Tyme. BusinessDay reported in August 2026 that Moove had also passed $1 billion.

What is Africa's most valuable fintech?

Flutterwave, valued at $3.25 billion after its Ripple-backed Series E in June 2026, according to TechCabal. OPay's implied value of about $3.1 billion comes from a shareholder's accounts rather than a funding round.

Is there a fintech unicorn in North Africa?

Yes, Egypt's MNT-Halan, valued at $1.4 billion in June 2026. Algeria's Yassir has been reported as a unicorn, but it has not officially disclosed such a valuation. Morocco and Tunisia have no fintech unicorn.

Are African unicorn valuations reliable?

They should be read with caution. Several, such as Wave's $1.7 billion and Chipper Cash's valuation, date from 2021 and have not been updated publicly. Africa: The Big Deal has noted that some may now be lower.
